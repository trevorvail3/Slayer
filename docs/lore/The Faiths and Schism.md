# The Faiths & the Schism

← [[World Bible (Home)]] · from [[The Creation Myth]] · into [[Factions]]

Mankind can't agree **why Vael slew Aldmar** — and since the answer decides what the
world *means*, the disagreement is not academic. It is the deepest fault line in
Aldermoor, and the city-states have bled over it for a thousand years. Four readings
dominate.

## 1. The Gift (the Sacrifice)
*Aldmar died so the world could live.* His death was necessary — willing, even — and
Vael was the midwife of creation, not a murderer. To the faithful of the Gift, all
life is borrowed from the Father's body and owed back in reverence. Orthodox, temple-
bound, priest-led. **Heresy = denying the Gift** (calling it murder, or calling it
nothing). Tends toward order, law, and zealotry. → the priest-city (see [[Factions]]).

## 2. The Betrayal
*Vael murdered Aldmar to reign alone.* The world is a crime scene. Two branches hate
each other as much as they hate everyone else:
- **The Cunning** — revere Vael's ruthlessness. The strong take; the clever win; the
  world belongs to whoever dares seize it. Ambition as virtue. → the tyrant-city.
- **The Bereaved** (the Father's Martyrs) — venerate the *murdered* Aldmar as a slain
  king to be avenged. Grim, martial, obsessed with strength and vengeance; some dream
  of "waking the Father." → the warrior-city and hard soldier-orders.

## 3. The Mourning (Grief)
*It was a tragedy.* Vael loved Aldmar and killed him in a moment she has wept for ever
since; the **Weeping Sea** is her tears mixed with his blood. Melancholic mystery
cults centered on death, loss, and rebirth. Gentle on the surface — but grief taken
too far bred the **[[The Dead and the Ossuary Cult|Ossuary Cult]]**, who decided that
if a god's death could be *undone into a world*, then death is a door, not an end.

## 4. The Deniers
*There are no gods.* The mountains are stone, the rivers water, the myth a story men
told to make sense of a hard land. Deniers lean rational, pragmatic, and secular —
philosophers, engineers, ancestor-honorers. To every faith above, they are the worst
heretics of all. → the free-thinker city.

## How to use this
- Each major **faction/city-state** wears one of these readings ([[Factions]]) — it
  gives them motive, allies, and enemies without a word of extra writing.
- The **player and the Bastion are unaligned** — a broken city free to lean any way,
  which makes faction reputation and choices meaningful (see [[Factions and The Town]]).
- The truth stays hidden. If the campaign ever *answers* the two questions
  ([[The Creation Myth]]), it should shatter or vindicate whole faiths — a
  world-shaking reveal, spent carefully.
